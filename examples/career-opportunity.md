# Example: Career Opportunity That Requires Public Alignment

## User prompt

> I have an opportunity to join a much stronger team. The opportunity could accelerate my career, but accepting it would require me to publicly distance myself from the people who originally supported me. The new team's future is promising but not guaranteed. Should I take it?

## What the skill should extract

### Decision-structure kernel

- **Goal:** gain resources and career upside without destroying long-term optionality.
- **Power:** the new team controls the immediate opportunity; the old relationship may control future trust and fallback options.
- **Information asymmetry:** the user cannot fully know the new team's future stability.
- **Reversibility:** public distancing may be hard to undo even if the new opportunity fails.
- **Reputation / alliance effect:** third parties may interpret the choice as a signal of loyalty or opportunism.
- **Tail risk:** the new team fails while the old network no longer trusts the user.

## Historical retrieval strategy

The agent should **not** search only for “career change” or “loyalty.” It should search for historical decisions involving:

- switching patrons or political alliances;
- receiving resources in exchange for a visible alignment signal;
- sacrificing an existing alliance for a stronger but uncertain sponsor;
- irreversible public commitments under incomplete information.

Candidate cases should then be similarity-scored. Only the 2–4 strongest matches should survive.

## Required analysis for each retained case

1. What was known before the decision?
2. What were the actor's plausible options?
3. Which motive is explicit in the primary text, and which is inferred?
4. What did the actor gain immediately?
5. What option value or trust did the actor lose?
6. Which costs became visible only later?
7. What part of the ancient institutional context makes the analogy imperfect?

## Modern translation

The final answer should convert the historical mechanisms back into questions such as:

- Can the user accept the opportunity without making an irreversible public break?
- Can the user delay the signaling cost until the new opportunity becomes more certain?
- Is the real price of the opportunity the work itself, or the destruction of a fallback relationship?
- Would a neutral third party interpret the move as normal career mobility or as betrayal?

This example intentionally contains no pre-written historical answer. A real run must retrieve and verify primary-text evidence before selecting cases.
